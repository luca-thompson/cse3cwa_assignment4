# Supermarket Automatic Simulator

This app runs a stock-only simulation in an IGA style supermarket in the Melbourne CBD. This simulation runs for exactly 60 days, reporting on results via a web application. The simulation has customers and varying daily demand, with the 'store brain' monitoring stock levels and ordering stock in accordance, so as to maintain stock levels. Overall and daily reports are generated by the application.

### Architecture and data flow

When a run is created it stores the customer model, brain rules, products, intial cash and random seed along with all other initialization variables. Following this a run is simulated, day by day, for 60 days. Each event is stored and logged into the database as it occurs, everything including stock movement, financial movement. These are then read back from the database to the frontend for display and reporting. The findings tab is all hardcoded into the frontend, it doesn't come from the backend.



Key files: `backend/` has `index.js` (routes), `db.js` (schema and triggers), `products.js`, `model.js`, `brain.js`, `simulate.js`, `seed.js`, `reports.js`, `assurance.js`, `check.js`, `checkProducts.js`. `frontend/src/` has `App.jsx` (navigation and run selector), `pages/` (one file per tab), `ProductHistory.jsx`, `api.js`, `format.js`, `styles.css`.

### Customer and demand model

Model configuration comes from model.js, on weekdays ~260 customers come through, with ~208 on weekends. This varies by 10% each day to create realistic demand fluctuation. A special 60% increase happens on a fictional festival saturday. Customers arrive with an hourly pattern; particularly through lunch and post-work periods on weekdays.

There are four types of customer; office worker, visitor, local resident and evening shopper. Each shows up at different types of hours and more or less based on weekday/vs not weekday. These customers can be on different types of shops; quick snack or drink, lunch, evening meal, grocery top-up or household errand. These change product count and type of product.

### Store brain rules

Store Brain rules come from brain.js. At the close of each day, it works out each product’s average daily sales over the last 7 days and its days of cover (usable stock divided by average sales). Batches that will expire before tomorrow’s opening don’t count as usable stock.

It records four conditions. Sold out is when there is no stock left. Low stock is when cover drops under 2 days, or under the product’s own target if that is smaller. An expiry alert happens when a batch expires within 2 days, and those units are sold first. Slow movers are checked weekly after Day 7, and a product with more than 45 days of cover is noted and never orderd again.

Products that are sold out or low get an order for up to 4 days of average sales, with a minimum of 6 units. Perishables are capped at 75% of their shelf life. If cash is limited, orders are ranked; sold out first, then lowest cover, then highest margin per unit. They are paid from operating cash in that order, and an order that can’t be fully afforded is reduced and noted as constrained. Orders arrive before opening the next day, and none are placed after Day 60.

Expired stock is removed at the start of its expiry day. A unit received on day 5 with shelf life 2 can be sold through day 5+2-1, 6, and opening stock counts as received on the first day.

I only used Claude by Anthropic for this assignment

I used the tool to draft initial versions of most of the assignments files, along with some project scaffolding. This includes the backend logic, and particularly the frontend and its componenets.

The tool's output was reviewed frequently and thoroughly to ensure it was correct and reasonable. Some examples of my changing its output are detailed here;
- Project scaffold was overcomplex, the tool reccomended an architechture that was unecessary; featuring a frontend that was served via the backend and a customer model with far more customer types and more granular shopping types.
- The tool attempted to insert as many graphs as it could into the frontend using the 'Victory' chart tool. I cut many back and decided recharts was a better fit given the size of the project.

### Known limitations

- Demand is not as variable as real life, its much too consistent
- no rent or utilities are calculated or simulated
- employees and wages not simulated
- no purchase process or checkouts, could be interesting to throttle checkout maximums.
- Max basket size is 6
- few customer and purchase types
- fift items is extremely low
